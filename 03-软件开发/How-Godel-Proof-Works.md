# How Gödel’s Proof Works

**Author:** Natalie Wolchover, Quanta Magazine, July 14, 2020

Gödel's incompleteness theorems (1931) showed any consistent axiomatic system for mathematics is incomplete and cannot prove its own consistency. This article provides an accessible walkthrough of the proof.

## Key Ideas

- **Gödel numbering**: Map every arithmetic formula/sequence to a unique integer by raising successive primes to symbol codes.
  - Example: `0 = 0` becomes `2^6 × 3^5 × 5^6 = 243,000,000`.
- **Arithmetization of metamathematics**: Statements about formulas become statements about numbers, letting a system talk about itself.
- **Self-reference**: A formula can assert "This statement is unprovable." If true, it's unprovable; if false, the system proves a falsehood.
- **Consequences**: No mathematical theory of everything; truth outruns provability. Undecidable examples: continuum hypothesis, halting problem, and even physics phenomena.

## Implications

- Hilbert's program of a complete, consistent foundation fails.
- What mathematicians can prove depends on starting axioms, not absolute ground truth.
- Gödel's ideas extend beyond math into computation and physics.
